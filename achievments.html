School Council

I have been elected as school council for the three years now (during year7, year8 and year9). My main goal has always been to bring the point of view of my classmates to the people who can make a change and solve their problems. Each year I have tried to do something unique, and to make the school a place where people want to come to. For example, in year seven I suggested to add an additional reward in the form of a raffle for those people with clean uniform cards (which means they followed all the uniform regulations). This gave one person, who had a clean uniform card, a chance to win a £25 gift voucher. In year 8 I suggested having a culture day known as "the world fair" where people dress up and display their cultures, celebrating how diverse the world is! All of my suggestions have been approved and put in place. The world fair is now an annual tradition for the high school and the raffle takes place at the end of every big term.


NKDC Student Body

I was also chosen to represent my school in NKDC's student body. I was asked to fill out a form, along with many others, and the teachers elected me to take part in this opportunity. We have a couple of meetings every year where we spend a whole school day discussing issues in our local district and gathering thoughts on how we can improve it. Our thoughts are then fed back to the head of the council, who decides which ideas they need to keep in mind moving forward. Each meeting is unique, covering different topics and perspectives, and every idea is to help make North Kesteven a better community.


Quadcopter Challenge

I also took part in the quadcopter challenge. In this challenge, I was part of a team of 5 students, and we all had to work together to design and build our own quadcopter! First, we had a qualification round where we had to create and display our idea for a quadcopter on the theme of “local heroes”. We chose the theme of search and rescue and named our drone “Search 4 Life”. We then made a PowerPoint about our ideas and sent it to the judges — luckily, we made it into the next round!

Next was the Regionals. This is when we got to build and fly our quadcopter. We first had a build day, where we were given the laser-cut template of the basic structure and the electronic components, and we had to assemble and test it. After that we had time for modifications — a key point in the process that got us into the next round. Some modifications included longer arms (for safety), longer legs with flat circular ends (for sturdier landings and battery protection), and extra roofs (to protect exposed electronics).

We also chose roles within the team. I was first positioned as the head pilot and got my own flier’s ID, but was then moved to head of modifications and co‑pilot. During the Finals of the Regionals, we came first as our drone model, presentation, and piloting exceeded the other teams. We then went to London for the Nationals. There were 13 other schools and cadet groups. In the end, we didn’t place, but we made it into the top 14 out of 400 schools competing from around the country and even Scotland!
